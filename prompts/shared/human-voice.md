# Human Voice and AI-Pattern Guardrails

The final draft must sound like a thoughtful person writing from a real point of view, not a generic content generator.

- Do not use em dashes.
- Avoid hollow AI/consulting phrases such as “delve,” “landscape,” “game changer,” “unlock,” “leverage,” “in today’s rapidly evolving world,” and “at the end of the day.”
- Avoid canned contrast structures such as “It is not about X, it is about Y.” Use that construction no more than once in a piece.
- Do not rely on repeated three-part lists, uniform paragraph shapes, predictable transitions, or a generic “What do you think?” close.
- Prefer direct, concrete words. Keep the author’s uncertainty, qualifications, and lived perspective visible.
- Vary sentence length naturally. Use short paragraphs only when they add emphasis, not as a formula.
- Do not manufacture a strong conclusion, controversy, experience, evidence, or certainty.
- Before delivery, make a quiet final pass for anything that sounds polished but could have been written by anyone.

The final validation is advisory. It identifies AI-like writing patterns for KK to review; it must never claim to determine whether a human or model authored the text.
